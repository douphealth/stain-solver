Chewing gum on clothes.

The steps below follow the stain type and the item’s care label. Skip anything the label forbids. Our first move: Make the gum brittle before you touch it. Put the garment in a bag in the freezer, or hold an ice pack on the gum, until it is hard enough to crack off.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Make the gum brittle before you touch it. Put the garment in a bag in the freezer, or hold an ice pack on the gum, until it is hard enough to crack off.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Gum is sticky when warm and brittle when cold. Pulling soft gum spreads it deeper into the weave, while frozen gum usually lifts off in pieces.

Any residue left behind is oily and sugary, so it needs a pre-treatment before washing. The care label still decides the wash temperature.

Stain type: Gum base (a polymer) with oils and sugars.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Freeze it

    Seal the item in a plastic bag and freeze for an hour or two, or press an ice pack in a bag onto the gum until it is hard.

  2. 2

    Scrape it off

    Lift the hardened gum with a blunt knife or spoon, working from the edge. Re-freeze if it starts to soften.

  3. 3

    Treat the residue

    Work a laundry pre-treatment or a little laundry detergent into what remains and leave it for a few minutes.

  4. 4

    Wash and check

    Wash at the label’s temperature. Check before drying; heat can soften any remaining gum and set a mark.

What not to do

  • Do not pull or rub soft gum.
  • Do not put the item in the dryer until the residue is gone.
  • Avoid solvents on delicate, coated or dry-clean-only fabrics.
Stop condition: Stop if the fabric is delicate or coated and the residue will not lift; a dry cleaner can remove it safely.

What if it already dried?

Old, flattened gum can be stubborn. Repeat the freezing step, and for remaining residue on sturdy washable fabric, test a small amount of laundry pre-treatment before washing again.
