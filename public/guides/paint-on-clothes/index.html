THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Work out the paint type from the tin or tube. Water-based paint (acrylic, latex, emulsion, poster paint) comes out far more easily while it is still wet; oil-based paint needs the solvent the paint label names.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Paint is not one stain. Water-based paints are pigment held in a plastic binder that sets as the water evaporates, so the first minutes matter most. Oil-based paints and enamels need a solvent rather than water.

The garment’s care label still controls water temperature and whether any solvent is safe. Delicate, dry-clean-only or structured items are best taken to a professional cleaner with the paint type noted.

Stain type: Binder and pigment. Once acrylic or latex paint has fully dried, removal is often only partial.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Scrape, don’t smear

    Lift off excess wet paint with a spoon or blunt knife, working from the edge inward so the mark does not spread.

  2. 2

    Water-based paint: rinse from the back

    While it is still wet, flush the back of the fabric under cool or lukewarm running water, then work in a little laundry detergent with your fingers and rinse again. Repeat until no more colour comes out.

  3. 3

    Dried water-based paint: test a solvent

    Soften the edge with isopropyl (rubbing) alcohol on a white cloth, after testing it on a hidden seam. Do not use alcohol on acetate, triacetate or modacrylic, and stop if dye moves.

  4. 4

    Oil-based paint: use the solvent the paint label names

    Blot with the thinner or mineral spirits the paint maker recommends, outdoors or with good ventilation and away from flames. Test first, then pre-treat with laundry detergent before washing.

  5. 5

    Wash, then check before drying

    Wash at the label’s temperature. Air-dry and inspect: a dryer’s heat can set any remaining paint.

What not to do

  • Do not let water-based paint dry before you start if you can avoid it.
  • Never put a solvent-soaked item in the dryer: flammable residue is a fire risk.
  • Do not mix solvents with bleach or other cleaners.
Stop condition: Stop if the care label forbids treatment, dye or finish lifts, or the fabric softens or puckers under a solvent.

What if it already dried?

Fully dried acrylic or latex paint has cured into a plastic film. Alcohol can soften the edges on some fabrics, but complete removal is not always possible, especially on loosely woven cotton.
