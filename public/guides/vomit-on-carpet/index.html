Vomit on carpet.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Put on disposable gloves and lift solids with paper towels or a dustpan, scooping inward so you don’t spread it.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Vomit combines stomach acid, protein and food dyes, which stain fibres and leave odour in the padding.

If someone is ill, especially with a stomach bug, hygiene matters as much as the stain. Ordinary spot cleaning removes the mark but does not necessarily disinfect carpet.

Stain type: Protein and acid, often with food dyes.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Protect yourself and lift solids

    Wear gloves, remove solids into a sealed bag, and ventilate the room.

  2. 2

    Absorb the moisture

    Sprinkle baking soda over the area, leave it to absorb liquid and odour, then vacuum it up once dry.

  3. 3

    Clean with an enzyme cleaner

    Apply an enzyme cleaner made for organic stains, after a hidden test, and leave it for the time on its label. Blot.

  4. 4

    Rinse lightly and dry

    Blot with a cloth dampened in cool water, then press dry and let the carpet air-dry fully.

What not to do

  • Do not use hot water or steam on fresh vomit; heat can set protein stains.
  • Do not rub; it pushes the stain deeper.
  • Never mix bleach with other cleaners, and do not use bleach on carpet unless its maker allows it.
Stop condition: Stop if the carpet colour changes in your test. For illness outbreaks, follow your local public-health cleaning guidance or use a professional.

What if it already dried?

Dried vomit should be loosened with a cool, damp cloth and lifted before any cleaner goes on. Repeat the enzyme step for lingering odour.
