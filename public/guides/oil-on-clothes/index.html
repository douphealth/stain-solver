Oil stains on clothes.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Blot off any surface oil with a paper towel, then cover the spot with baking soda before it spreads further into the fibres.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Oil doesn't mix with water, so a plain rinse just moves it around. A dry absorbent pulls up the free oil first, then a grease-cutting dish liquid lets water carry the rest away.

Heat sets oil. A mark that looks faint when wet can become permanent in the dryer, so always check it after washing and before any heat.

Stain type: Oil and grease.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Blot and absorb

    Blot without rubbing, then cover the stain with baking soda. Leave it for 15 to 30 minutes and brush it off.

  2. 2

    Work in clear dish liquid

    Where the care label allows washing, rub a few drops of clear dish liquid into the stain from both sides with your fingers or a soft brush. Leave it for a few minutes.

  3. 3

    Wash warm

    Wash at the warmest temperature the care label allows, with your usual laundry detergent.

  4. 4

    Check before drying

    Inspect the area in good light. If any mark remains, repeat the dish liquid step and wash again before drying.

What not to do

  • Do not tumble-dry or iron until the mark is completely gone.
  • Do not rub oil with a wet cloth first; it spreads.
  • Do not wash hotter than the care label allows.
Stop condition: Stop and use a professional cleaner if the item is dry-clean only, silk, leather or suede, or if the dish liquid changes the colour in a hidden test.

What if it already dried?

Old, dried oil marks often need two or three dish-liquid and wash cycles. Avoid the dryer between attempts.
