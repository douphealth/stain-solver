Permanent marker on clothes.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Put a folded paper towel under the mark, then dab rubbing alcohol on from the back so the ink is pushed out into the towel, not through the garment.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Permanent marker ink is designed to resist water, so plain washing usually sets it. Alcohol dissolves many permanent inks, which is why it is the usual first step.

Alcohol can also move fabric dye and damages some fibres, including acetate and triacetate. Test on a hidden seam and stop if colour comes away.

Stain type: Solvent-based dye ink.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Test the alcohol

    Dab isopropyl (rubbing) alcohol on a hidden seam with a white cloth. Continue only if the fabric’s colour does not transfer.

  2. 2

    Set up a blotting layer

    Lay the stained area face down on a folded paper towel or white cloth.

  3. 3

    Dab from the back

    Dab alcohol onto the back of the mark. Move the paper towel to a clean spot as the ink transfers, and keep going until no more ink lifts.

  4. 4

    Rinse, pre-treat, wash

    Rinse with water, rub in laundry detergent, then wash at the label’s temperature. Air-dry and check before using a dryer.

What not to do

  • Do not rub the mark; it spreads the ink.
  • Do not use alcohol on acetate, triacetate or modacrylic.
  • Keep alcohol away from flames and use it in a ventilated room.
Stop condition: Stop if the fabric’s own colour lifts, or the fibre softens or changes texture.

What if it already dried?

Older marker stains respond the same way but need more repetitions. Alcohol-based hand sanitizer can be used as a substitute after the same hidden test. Some ink may remain.

Before you treat it

Some markers are labelled “permanent on most surfaces” and resist removal from fabric. Expect fading, not always full removal.
