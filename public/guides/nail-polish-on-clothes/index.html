Nail polish on clothes.

The steps below follow the stain type and the item’s care label. Skip anything the label forbids. Our first move: Blot wet polish with a paper towel without rubbing, then scrape off any thick blobs with a blunt edge.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Blot wet polish with a paper towel without rubbing, then scrape off any thick blobs with a blunt edge.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Nail polish is a lacquer that hardens quickly. Remover dissolves it, but acetone also dissolves acetate, triacetate and modacrylic fabrics and can lift dyes.

Check the fibre label first. Non-acetone remover is gentler but slower; either one needs a hidden test.

Stain type: Lacquer with pigment.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Blot and scrape

    Blot wet polish and scrape off excess. Don't spread it.

  2. 2

    Test the remover

    Read the fibre label, then test remover on a hidden seam. Avoid acetone entirely on acetate, triacetate and modacrylic.

  3. 3

    Dab from the back

    Lay the stain face down on a paper towel and dab remover onto the back so the polish transfers to the towel. Move to a clean spot as it lifts.

  4. 4

    Rinse, pre-treat, wash

    Rinse, work in laundry detergent, and wash at the label's temperature.

What not to do

  • Never use acetone on acetate, triacetate or modacrylic.
  • Do not rub; it spreads the lacquer.
  • Use remover in a ventilated room, away from flames.
Stop condition: Stop if the fabric melts, puckers or loses colour in the test.

What if it already dried?

Dried polish takes repeated short dabs of remover. Some colour may remain on light fabrics.
