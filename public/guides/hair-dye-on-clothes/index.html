Hair dye on clothes.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Rinse the stain from the back under cold running water immediately. Hair dye is designed to bind permanently, so the first few minutes matter most.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Permanent and semi-permanent hair dyes are made to bond to protein fibres like hair, and they also bond to fabric. Once a dye mark dries, it is often permanent, especially on wool and silk.

Cold water, detergent and, where the label allows, an oxygen bleach soak give the best chance on washable items.

Stain type: Dye.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Rinse cold from the back

    Flush the stain with cold water until the water runs clear.

  2. 2

    Work in liquid detergent

    Rub laundry detergent into the mark, leave it for a few minutes, and rinse.

  3. 3

    Test rubbing alcohol on remaining dye

    Dab isopropyl alcohol on a hidden area first. If the fabric holds its colour, blot the stain with alcohol on a white cloth.

  4. 4

    Soak if the label allows

    For whites and colourfast items whose bleach symbol permits it, soak in oxygen bleach made to the product’s directions, then wash.

What not to do

  • Do not use hot water; it helps the dye bond.
  • Do not tumble-dry until you are sure the mark is gone.
  • Never mix bleach with other cleaners.
Stop condition: Stop if the garment’s own colour lifts, or it is wool, silk or dry-clean only.

What if it already dried?

Dried hair dye is often permanent. Repeat the alcohol and oxygen-bleach steps where allowed, but expect the mark to fade rather than vanish.
