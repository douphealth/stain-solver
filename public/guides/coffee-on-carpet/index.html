THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Blot as much coffee as possible with a white towel, pressing straight down. Don’t rub, and don’t pour more water on yet.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Coffee contains tannins that dye fibres, and milk or cream add protein and fat. Quick blotting removes most of it before it binds.

Carpet holds liquid in its backing and padding, so the aim is to clean the fibres while adding as little water as possible.

Stain type: Tannin, plus protein and fat when milk is added.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Blot it up

    Press dry white towels onto the spill, working from the outside in, until no more transfers.

  2. 2

    Apply a mild detergent solution

    Mix a little clear, dye-free dish liquid into cool water. Test on a hidden area, then apply a small amount with a cloth and blot.

  3. 3

    Rinse and blot again

    Blot with a cloth dampened in plain water to remove the detergent, then press dry with towels.

  4. 4

    Use a carpet spot cleaner if needed

    For a lingering mark, use a spot cleaner suited to your carpet fibre, following its label, after a hidden test.

What not to do

  • Do not rub; it frays the pile and spreads the stain.
  • Do not over-wet the carpet; moisture in the padding can cause rings and odour.
  • Do not mix cleaners together.
Stop condition: Stop if colour transfers to your cloth or the pile changes texture; call a carpet professional.

What if it already dried?

A dried coffee stain needs the detergent step repeated several times. Old tannin marks may fade rather than disappear.
