Mould on clothes.

The steps below follow the stain type and the item’s care label. Skip anything the label forbids. Our first move: Take the item outside and brush off loose mould with a stiff brush, so spores don't spread indoors. Consider a mask if you are sensitive to mould.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Take the item outside and brush off loose mould with a stiff brush, so spores don't spread indoors. Consider a mask if you are sensitive to mould.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Mould and mildew grow on fabric that stays damp, such as clothes left in the washer or stored in a humid place. They leave dark spots and a musty smell.

Heat, detergent and, where the label allows, bleach remove both the growth and the odour. Fabric that has been eaten into may not recover.

Stain type: Biological growth.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Brush it off outdoors

    Brush the dry growth off outside, away from open windows.

  2. 2

    Pre-treat the spots

    Work laundry detergent into the marks and leave it for 10–15 minutes.

  3. 3

    Wash hot, with bleach if allowed

    Wash at the hottest temperature the care label allows. Add oxygen bleach for colours, or chlorine bleach only for whites whose label allows it.

  4. 4

    Dry completely

    Dry fully, in sunlight if possible. Don't put the item away until it is completely dry.

What not to do

  • Do not shake mouldy items indoors.
  • Never mix chlorine bleach with ammonia, vinegar or other cleaners.
  • Do not store clothes damp, which lets mould return.
Stop condition: Stop and discard if the fabric is weakened or holed. Seek professional help for valuable or dry-clean-only items.

What if it already dried?

Old, set mildew spots may leave permanent marks even after the growth is gone. Repeat washing usually removes the smell.
