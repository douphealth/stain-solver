Chocolate on synthetic carpet.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Lift excess solids or oily material with a spoon or the edge of a dull tool. Do not rub.

Why synthetic carpet changes the answer

This guidance covers synthetic, colourfast carpets rather than wool, silk or antique rugs. Spot-testing and avoiding saturation help protect pile and backing. Chocolate combines solids and oils: lift the excess before cleaning.

Stain type: Fat + solids + pigment. Chocolate combines fats and particles. Lift solids first, then treat residual oils on washable fabrics.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Lift or blot the spill

    Lift excess solids or oily material with a spoon or the edge of a dull tool. Do not rub.

  2. 2

    Check carpet fibre and maker instructions

    Proceed only with synthetic, colourfast carpet where the carpet manufacturer allows damp spot cleaning. Wool, silk or unknown rugs need specialist advice.

  3. 3

    Mild spot treatment — if approved

    IICRC suggests a few drops of liquid dish soap in 1 cup (about 240 mL) of water. Patch-test, then apply with a barely damp white cloth. Blot from the outside inward; do not saturate.

  4. 4

    Remove residue and dry

    Blot with another cloth dampened with clean water, then press with dry towels. Avoid leaving soapy residue or wetting the carpet backing.

  5. 5

    Stop if it spreads or changes

    Stop for dye movement, rings, sticky residue or persistent marks. Consult an IICRC-qualified cleaner.

What not to do

  • Do not rub, scrub or abrade the spot.
  • Never mix bleach with another cleaner, including acids or ammonia.
  • Do not skip a hidden-place dye/finish test.
Stop condition: Stop for wool, silk, antique or unknown rug fibres, dye movement, residue or water rings.

What if it already dried or went through the dryer?

Avoid spreading melted chocolate by rubbing. A dried or heat-exposed stain can require different handling, and removal may be incomplete. In the interactive result, also select “already machine-dried or ironed” if applicable. The care-label restriction still takes priority.
