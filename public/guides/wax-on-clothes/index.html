Candle wax on clothes.

The steps below follow the stain type and the item’s care label. Skip anything the label forbids. Our first move: Let the wax harden fully, or speed it up with an ice pack, then flake off as much as you can before using any heat.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Let the wax harden fully, or speed it up with an ice pack, then flake off as much as you can before using any heat.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Most wax sits on the surface and cracks off once cold. Only a thin film soaks into the fibres, and gentle heat with absorbent paper can draw it out.

Ironing is only safe where the garment's iron symbol allows it, and on the lowest setting that melts the wax. Coloured candles can leave dye behind that needs a separate pre-treatment.

Stain type: Wax, sometimes with dye.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Harden and scrape

    Chill the wax with an ice pack, then flake it off with a blunt knife.

  2. 2

    Lift the film with paper and low heat

    If the label allows ironing, sandwich the area between plain paper towels or brown paper and press briefly with a low, dry iron. Move to clean paper as it absorbs wax.

  3. 3

    Treat any colour

    Rub laundry detergent or a stain pre-treatment into any dye mark and leave it for a few minutes.

  4. 4

    Wash and check

    Wash at the label's temperature and check before drying.

What not to do

  • Do not iron fabrics whose label forbids ironing, or use high heat on synthetics.
  • Do not use printed paper; the ink can transfer.
  • Do not tumble-dry until all wax is gone; it can melt onto the drum.
Stop condition: Stop if the fabric shines, puckers or melts under the iron.

What if it already dried?

Old wax is easier than fresh: it's already hard. The steps are the same.
