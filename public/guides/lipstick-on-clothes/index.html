Lipstick on clothes.

The steps below follow the stain type and the item’s care label. Skip anything the label forbids. Our first move: Scrape off any surface lipstick with a blunt knife, lifting it away from the fabric rather than wiping across it.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Scrape off any surface lipstick with a blunt knife, lifting it away from the fabric rather than wiping across it.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Lipstick combines wax, oil and strong pigment. Water alone beads off it, and heat melts the wax deeper into the fibres.

An oil-cutting detergent tackles the wax and oil first; long-wear formulas may also need a solvent such as rubbing alcohol, after a test.

Stain type: Wax and oil with pigment.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Lift the excess

    Scrape gently with a blunt knife. Don’t rub.

  2. 2

    Break down the oil

    Work a little laundry detergent or clear dish liquid into the mark with your fingers and leave it for a few minutes.

  3. 3

    Long-wear lipstick: test rubbing alcohol

    If colour remains, dab isopropyl alcohol from the back onto a paper towel after testing a hidden seam.

  4. 4

    Wash and check

    Wash at the warmest temperature the care label allows. Air-dry and check before tumble-drying.

What not to do

  • Do not use hot water or a dryer before the stain is gone; heat melts the wax in.
  • Do not use alcohol on acetate or triacetate.
  • Do not rub the mark across the fabric.
Stop condition: Stop if colour lifts from the fabric during a test, or the item is silk or dry-clean only.

What if it already dried?

Set lipstick needs the detergent step repeated, sometimes with a brief soak. Marks that went through the dryer may only fade.
