Tree sap on clothes.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Harden the sap with an ice pack and lift off as much as you can with a blunt knife before using any liquid.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Sap is a sticky resin that water doesn't dissolve. Alcohol breaks it down, which is why rubbing alcohol is the usual first solvent.

Alcohol can move some dyes and damages acetate and triacetate, so test on a hidden seam first.

Stain type: Resin.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Harden and scrape

    Hold an ice pack on the sap until it is firm, then scrape the excess off with a blunt knife.

  2. 2

    Test, then dissolve with alcohol

    Test isopropyl (rubbing) alcohol on a hidden seam. If the colour holds, dab it onto the sap with a white cloth until the residue softens and lifts.

  3. 3

    Pre-treat

    Rub laundry detergent into the area and leave it for a few minutes.

  4. 4

    Wash and check

    Wash at the warmest temperature the care label allows. Check before drying, because heat can set any remaining resin.

What not to do

  • Do not rub soft sap; it spreads.
  • Do not use alcohol on acetate, triacetate or modacrylic.
  • Do not tumble-dry until the mark is gone.
Stop condition: Stop if colour lifts in the test or the fabric is delicate or dry-clean only.

What if it already dried?

Hardened, old sap may need several alcohol applications. Alcohol-based hand sanitizer can substitute after the same test.
