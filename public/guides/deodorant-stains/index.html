Deodorant stains.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Tell the two kinds apart. Fresh white marks on the surface usually brush off; yellow, stiff underarm stains are a build-up that needs pre-treating before washing.

Why this stain needs its own approach

White marks are undissolved deodorant sitting on the fabric. Yellowing builds up over many wears, as antiperspirant ingredients combine with sweat and body oils, and heat from the dryer or iron makes it harder to shift.

Coloured garments need colour-safe products only. Check the care label before any soaking or bleaching.

Stain type: Residue (white marks) or a mineral and oil build-up (yellow marks).

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Fresh white marks: rub them away dry

    Rub the mark with a clean, dry piece of similar fabric, a dry sponge or a soft brush. Most surface residue disappears.

  2. 2

    Yellow build-up: pre-treat

    Work laundry detergent or an enzyme pre-treatment into the underarm area and leave it for the time on its label.

  3. 3

    Soak if the label allows

    For washable whites and colourfast items whose bleach symbol allows it, soak in an oxygen bleach solution made to the product’s directions, then wash.

  4. 4

    Wash and air-dry

    Wash at the label’s temperature and air-dry. Repeat before drying if any yellow remains.

What not to do

  • Do not use chlorine bleach on underarm stains; it can react and darken the yellowing.
  • Do not tumble-dry or iron until the stain has gone.
  • Do not mix bleach with any other cleaner.
Stop condition: Stop if colour lifts during a test, or the fabric is silk, wool or dry-clean only.

What if it already dried?

Long-standing yellow marks may need several pre-treat and wash cycles, and may only fade partly. Letting deodorant dry fully before dressing helps prevent new marks.
