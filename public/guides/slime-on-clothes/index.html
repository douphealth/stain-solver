Slime on clothes.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Pull or scrape off as much slime as you can before using any liquid. If it is soft and stringy, freeze the garment first so it breaks away instead of stretching.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Most homemade and shop-bought slime is a glue-based gel. Rubbing it while soft pushes it into the weave, and coloured or glitter slime can leave dye and sparkle behind after the gel is gone.

White vinegar is widely used to loosen the gel so it rinses out, but it is still a cleaner: test it on a hidden seam, and follow the care label for the wash that follows.

Stain type: Gel, often with dye or glitter.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Remove the bulk

    Pick off loose slime, then scrape with a blunt knife or spoon. If it smears, put the item in a bag in the freezer for an hour and scrape again.

  2. 2

    Test, then apply white vinegar

    Test white vinegar on a hidden area. If the colour holds, soak the remaining slime with it for a few minutes until it softens.

  3. 3

    Work it loose and rinse

    Rub gently with a soft brush or your fingers, then rinse with warm water if the care label allows. Repeat until no gel remains.

  4. 4

    Pre-treat colour, then wash

    Rub laundry detergent into any coloured mark, leave it for a few minutes, then wash at the label’s temperature. Air-dry and check before using a dryer.

What not to do

  • Do not tumble-dry until all slime and colour are gone.
  • Never mix vinegar with bleach: the combination releases toxic chlorine gas.
  • Do not rub soft slime outward; it spreads into a larger mark.
Stop condition: Stop if colour lifts in the vinegar test, or the item is silk, wool or dry-clean only.

What if it already dried?

Dried slime is crusty rather than sticky. Soak it in white vinegar (after a test) until it softens, brush it out, then pre-treat and wash.
