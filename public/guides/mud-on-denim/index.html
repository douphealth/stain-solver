Mud on denim.

For a washable, colourfast item, the steps below follow the stain type and fabric label. Skip anything the label forbids. Our first move: Let ordinary mud dry, then lift it without grinding in the grit.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Let ordinary mud dry, then lift it without grinding in the grit.

Why denim changes the answer

Indigo-dyed denim can lose colour under forceful treatment. A gentle first move is more sensible than vigorous scrubbing. Wet mud spreads; it is often easier to lift once it has dried.

Stain type: Mineral particles / soil. Allowing ordinary mud to dry can make its gritty solids easier to lift without pushing them into fibres.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Lift the dry grit

    If mud is wet, allow it to dry. Gently brush off loose soil, working without grinding it into the yarn.

  2. 2

    Apply a labelled laundry pretreatment

    Use a little liquid laundry detergent on the mark only if fabric and product labels permit. Work gently; wait exactly the product-label contact time, then rinse.

  3. 3

    Launder once, then inspect

    Wash at the label-approved temperature with laundry detergent. Turn the garment inside out if its label advises it, to help reduce colour loss. Check the stain while the garment is still damp; air-dry rather than tumble-dry until it is gone.

What not to do

  • Do not iron or tumble-dry while the mark remains.
  • Never mix bleach with another cleaner, including acids or ammonia.
  • Do not skip a hidden-place dye/finish test.
Stop condition: Stop if the care label forbids treatment, dye transfers, fibre weakens, or cleaning spreads the mark.

What if it already dried or went through the dryer?

Brush dried mud gently without grinding grit into the fibres. A dried or heat-exposed stain can require different handling, and removal may be incomplete. In the interactive result, also select “already machine-dried or ironed” if applicable. The care-label restriction still takes priority.
