Blood on sheets.

The steps below follow the stain type and the item’s care label. Skip anything the label forbids. Our first move: Rinse the stain from the back with cold water as soon as you can. Heat makes blood bind to the fibres, so keep hot water and the dryer away until it is gone.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Rinse the stain from the back with cold water as soon as you can. Heat makes blood bind to the fibres, so keep hot water and the dryer away until it is gone.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Most sheets are cotton, linen or polyester blends that tolerate laundering, but the care label still sets the temperature and which products are allowed. Coloured and printed sheets need the same care as coloured clothes.

Blood is a protein stain. Cold water and an enzyme pre-treatment work with it; hot water and heat cook the protein into the fibres.

Stain type: Protein. Fresh blood rinses out far more easily than dried or heat-set blood.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Protect your hands

    Wear gloves when handling another person’s blood or blood from an unknown source.

  2. 2

    Flush with cold water from the back

    Strip the sheet and hold the stained area under cold running water, pushing the blood back out the way it came in.

  3. 3

    Pre-treat the protein

    If both labels allow it, apply an enzyme laundry pre-treatment and leave it for the time on its label. Do not use enzyme products on silk or wool bedding.

  4. 4

    For white cotton sheets only: hydrogen peroxide, after a test

    3% hydrogen peroxide can lift a stubborn mark from white cotton. Test on a hem first, dab it on, let it fizz, rinse well. It can lighten colours, so avoid it on coloured or printed sheets.

  5. 5

    Wash cold, then inspect before drying

    Wash on a cool cycle the label allows. Check the stain while the sheet is damp and air-dry it if any mark remains.

What not to do

  • Do not start with hot water or put a stained sheet in the dryer.
  • Never mix bleach with hydrogen peroxide, ammonia or other cleaners.
  • Do not use hydrogen peroxide on coloured sheets without a hidden test.
Stop condition: Stop if the care label forbids treatment, colour lifts, or the fabric weakens.

What if it already dried?

Dried blood needs a longer soak: cold water with an enzyme detergent for an hour or more, then a gentle rub between the fingers. Heat-set blood may only partly fade.
