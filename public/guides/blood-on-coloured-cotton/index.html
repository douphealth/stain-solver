Blood on coloured cotton.

For a washable, colourfast item, the steps below follow the stain type and fabric label. Skip anything the label forbids. Our first move: Avoid bare-handed contact with blood. For a small spill you can handle safely, use protective gloves and blot without rubbing; otherwise seek professional help.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Avoid bare-handed contact with blood. For a small spill you can handle safely, use protective gloves and blot without rubbing; otherwise seek professional help.

Why coloured cotton changes the answer

Coloured cotton needs a dye-transfer check. Even a cleaner that removes a stain may lighten the garment, so start with a hidden seam test. Blood is a protein-based stain, so cooler water is a sensible first move.

Stain type: Protein. Fresh blood is protein-rich. Hot water at the beginning can make fresh blood harder to remove.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Blood exposure precaution

    Blood from another person or an unknown source can pose infection risks. Avoid direct contact; use appropriate gloves and precautions. For a substantial or potentially infectious spill, stop and contact a qualified professional.

  2. 2

    Rinse with cool water

    Flush the back of washable fabric with cool running water. Never start fresh blood with hot water.

  3. 3

    Pre-treat protein residue

    If both labels permit, use an enzyme-containing laundry pretreatment for the time printed on its label. Rinse, then wash. Do not use this on wool or silk.

  4. 4

    Launder once, then inspect

    Wash at the label-approved temperature with laundry detergent. Wash with similar colours; skip bleaching products unless both labels permit them. Check the stain while the garment is still damp; air-dry rather than tumble-dry until it is gone.

What not to do

  • For fresh blood, do not start with hot water.
  • Never mix bleach with another cleaner, including acids or ammonia.
  • Do not skip a hidden-place dye/finish test.
Stop condition: Stop if the care label forbids treatment, dye transfers, fibre weakens, or cleaning spreads the mark.

What if it already dried or went through the dryer?

For blood that may be infectious, use protective precautions; do not handle another person’s blood without appropriate protection. A dried or heat-exposed stain can require different handling, and removal may be incomplete. In the interactive result, also select “already machine-dried or ironed” if applicable. The care-label restriction still takes priority.
