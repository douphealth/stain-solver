THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Soak up as much urine as you can right away: press layers of paper towels or an old towel onto the spot and stand on them.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Urine soaks through the pile into the backing and padding. The visible mark is only part of the problem: odour left behind can draw a dog back to the same spot.

Enzyme cleaners made for pet urine break down the compounds that cause the smell, but they need time and contact to work.

Stain type: Urine, with odour that lingers in the padding.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Absorb it

    Blot with thick layers of paper towels or a towel, pressing hard, until little more comes up.

  2. 2

    Rinse lightly

    Blot the area with a cloth dampened in cool water, then absorb again. Don’t flood the carpet.

  3. 3

    Apply a pet-urine enzyme cleaner

    Test it on a hidden area, then apply enough to reach as deep as the urine did. Leave it for the time on its label.

  4. 4

    Let it air-dry

    Blot off the excess and let the area dry naturally with good airflow. Keep pets off it until dry.

What not to do

  • Do not use ammonia-based cleaners; their smell can resemble urine and attract the dog back.
  • Do not use a steam cleaner or heat; it can set the stain and odour.
  • Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners.
Stop condition: Stop if the carpet changes colour in the test, or the odour keeps returning; the padding may need professional treatment.

What if it already dried?

Old urine spots often smell more when they get damp. They need a generous enzyme application that reaches the padding, and repeat treatments. A UV torch can help find old spots.
