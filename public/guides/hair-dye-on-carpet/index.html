THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Blot fresh dye straight away with a white cloth, working from the outside in. Hair dye is designed to bond with fibres, so speed matters.

Why this stain needs its own approach

Permanent hair dye develops colour that binds to fibres, much as it does to hair. Fresh dye is far easier to lift than dye that has developed for an hour or more.

Wool carpets take dye readily and are easily damaged by alcohol and peroxide, so they're best left to a professional.

Stain type: Developing dye.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Blot, don't rub

    Blot with a white absorbent cloth, outside in, until no more colour transfers.

  2. 2

    Apply a dish liquid and vinegar solution

    Mix one tablespoon of clear dish liquid and one tablespoon of white vinegar into two cups of warm water. Test it on a hidden spot, then sponge it on and blot repeatedly.

  3. 3

    Try alcohol on synthetic carpet

    If colour remains on nylon or polyester carpet, test isopropyl alcohol on a hidden spot, then dab it on and blot.

  4. 4

    Rinse and dry

    Sponge with plain cool water, blot dry, and let the area dry fully before walking on it.

What not to do

  • Do not use chlorine bleach; it removes carpet colour too.
  • Do not use alcohol or peroxide on wool carpet.
  • Do not scrub; it frays the pile.
Stop condition: Stop if the carpet colour lifts in a test, or the carpet is wool, silk or a natural fibre.

What if it already dried?

Set hair dye may not come out fully. A professional carpet cleaner is the safest next step.
