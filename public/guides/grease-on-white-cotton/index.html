Grease & oil on white cotton.

THE SHORT ANSWER

START HERE

Lift excess solids or oily material with a spoon or the edge of a dull tool. Do not rub.

Why white cotton changes the answer

White cotton is usually launderable, but the care symbol still controls wash temperature and which products may be used. White fabric does not automatically tolerate chlorine bleach. Oil needs a different approach than water-based drink stains.

Stain type: Fat / oil. Oil does not rinse away like a water-based drink. Use a fabric-compatible detergent before laundering.

A careful step-by-step approach

  1. 1

    Lift excess oil or pigment

    Dab away oily residue and any loose material with a fresh white towel or spoon. Avoid spreading it.

  2. 2

    Use a laundry detergent intended for oily marks

    If permitted by the care label, put a small amount of liquid laundry detergent on the spot. Use the product-label quantity and contact time. Rinse before laundering.

  3. 3

    Launder once, then inspect

    Wash at the label-approved temperature with laundry detergent. If considering an oxygen laundry product for a persistent mark, check BOTH the textile’s bleach symbol and the cleaner instructions; never assume white means bleach-safe. Check the stain while the garment is still damp; air-dry rather than tumble-dry until it is gone.

What not to do

  • Do not iron or tumble-dry while the mark remains.
  • Never mix bleach with another cleaner, including acids or ammonia.
  • Do not skip a hidden-place dye/finish test.
Stop condition: Stop if the care label forbids treatment, dye transfers, fibre weakens, or cleaning spreads the mark.

What if it already dried or went through the dryer?

Extra heat can help set stains or damage some fabrics; the care label sets the limit. A dried or heat-exposed stain can require different handling, and removal may be incomplete. In the interactive result, also select “already machine-dried or ironed” if applicable. The care-label restriction still takes priority.
